## Stronger action evidence for Service

The Charity Commission files include the charity's accounts and annual reporting for periods ending 31 March 2024 and 31 March 2025. The accounts name Burnham's contribution of £12,375 in the former period and include a £15,125 comparative figure. The later report names £11,050 and separately explains a £10,000 Sky speaker-fee contribution on his behalf. The latter is not added as another direct personal payment or treated as a continuous percentage of salary. These documents are recipient-filed financial records; the review does not label them personal bank statements or proof of all contribution years. No claim that all payments were independently audited is made.

For Obama, the March 2010 press announcement alone would establish intention. The scanned correspondence is stronger: the signed instruction asks for the award to go directly to charities, and the Nobel Foundation's signed confirmation dated 12 April says the transfer took place on 18 March 2010. The published tax explanation distinguishes exclusion of the prize from income from a charitable deduction, preventing an unsupported claim that an additional deduction was taken. This is documentary evidence of a specific choice involving financial advantage; it does not prove the absence of other conflicts or private motives.

## Crisis evidence and its limits

The new crisis materials concern words delivered during acute events rather than solely later apologies or reform announcements. They permit examination of instructions, acknowledgement of uncertainty or distress, reliance on expertise as stated by the speaker, public solidarity and explanation of choices.

The review does not diagnose internal calm from a transcript. An assertion that advice was followed remains the speaker's account until independently corroborated. Clarity of instructions does not establish that restrictions were timely, that public confidence improved or that emergency operations were effective. Existing pandemic, policing, implementation and civilian-harm material therefore remains in the record. The later coalition and budget material for Scholz remains qualifying context, rather than being erased by one reassuring address.

## People: direct contact is stronger than indirect attribution

The Füssen and Bendorf accounts identify citizens' questions and Scholz's answers about practical concerns. This corrects reliance on a separate Foreign Office process as evidence of his personal listening. Both sources are government-selected accounts of managed events, however. They do not establish representativeness, deep empathy, fulfilment of the promises or a causal link between listening and policy change. No successful policy outcome is inferred.

## The unresolved Service entry

Scholz's archived parliamentary record identifies functions and a law-partnership interest. This is personal disclosure evidence and is retained. It does not establish whether he recused himself, disposed of an interest, refused a benefit or managed an actual competing-duty decision during the chancellorship.

Targeted searches for the declared partnership, interests management and recusal did not yield a suitable documented case in this pass. Earlier Hamburg or finance-minister controversies are not imported as scored chancellorship behaviour. A declared interest is not itself wrongdoing, and no adverse case found is not evidence of no adverse conduct. **The gap remains open.**
