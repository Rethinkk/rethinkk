# Olaf Scholz — draft leadership profile

**For human research review — not an official RETHINKK assessment.** Prepared 17 October 2026.

Office: Federal Chancellor, Germany. Assessment scope: 2021-12-08 to 2025-05-06. Methodology: six original dimensions, version 1.0 proposed and unapproved. See the [coverage audit](../coverage-audit.md) for temporal and domain gaps.

No proposed numerical score is assigned where a material evidence gap remains. All totals are unassessed. The qualitative conclusions below are newly written research interpretations, not recovered prototype reasoning or independent reviewer findings.

## People

**Draft judgement:** The security statement includes household pressures, while research on the wider strategy process documents citizen participation and limits on that participation. This supports an institutional question rather than a personal empathy judgement.

### Supporting evidence

- **OS03-C1** — Scholz announced a security-policy shift following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, including a defence fund, commitments to allies and measures on energy security and household pressures. [OS03](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-en/service/archive/policy-statement-by-olaf-scholz-chancellor-of-the-federal-republic-of-germany-and-member-of-the-german-bundestag-27-february-2022-in-berlin-2008378); Fourth policy area: defence capability; energy security and relief package.

### Adverse or contradictory evidence

No directly attributable item admitted in this category. This is an evidence gap, not a finding that none exists.


### Qualifying evidence

- **NOS07-C1** — The study describes citizen participation in the Foreign Office’s strategy process and argues that control techniques limited its opening. [NOS07](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11615-024-00548-9); German and English abstracts.

**Uncertainty and attribution:** The participation study concerns the Foreign Office, not proof of Scholz’s individual listening. Affected-community evidence and a direct decision-to-consultation trail remain insufficient.

**Evidence sufficiency:** enough for the bounded qualitative discussion above; insufficient for a complete-period numerical assessment. **Proposed score: Not assessed.** Material coverage, attribution or interpretation gaps described above remain unresolved. A number would imply a whole-period judgement not yet supported.

## Process

**Draft judgement:** The invalid supplementary budget is a concrete institutional failure. Public acceptance of the decision, use of a confidence procedure and documented transfer provide important countervailing evidence of working within constitutional processes.

### Supporting evidence

- **NOS01-C1** — Scholz accepted the constitutional court decision as politically binding, described adjustments and framed the implications in terms of crisis management. [NOS01](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/service/newsletter-und-abos/bulletin/regierungserklaerung-von-bundeskanzler-olaf-scholz-2245460); Opening court-response passages.
- **NOS03-C1** — The Bundestag records Scholz’s Article 68 confidence motion and its defeat on 16 December 2024. [NOS03](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2024/kw47-vertrauensfrage-1030198); Motion, vote result and constitutional procedure.
- **OS02-C1** — The official record documents the handover from Scholz to Merz on 6 May 2025 and includes their statements. [OS02](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/amtsuebergaber-scholz-merz-2345472); Dated introduction and handover statements.

### Adverse or contradictory evidence

- **OS04-C1** — The Court’s press release states that the Second Supplementary Budget Act 2021 was incompatible with specified Basic Law provisions and void; the operative judgement confirms this. [OS04](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2023/bvg23-101.html); Press release opening; V06 operative judgement.

### Qualifying evidence

- **NOS07-C1** — The study describes citizen participation in the Foreign Office’s strategy process and argues that control techniques limited its opening. [NOS07](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11615-024-00548-9); German and English abstracts.

**Uncertainty and attribution:** Acceptance after an adverse ruling does not erase it. Full submissions, legal advice and the allocation of responsibility inside the coalition require closer reading; do not infer dishonesty.

**Evidence sufficiency:** enough for the bounded qualitative discussion above; insufficient for a complete-period numerical assessment. **Proposed score: Not assessed.** Material coverage, attribution or interpretation gaps described above remain unresolved. A number would imply a whole-period judgement not yet supported.
